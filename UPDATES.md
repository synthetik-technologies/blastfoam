## 1. New Thermodynamic Models & Equations of State

### 1.1 Multicomponent & Reacting Phase Fluid Thermodynamics
- **`multicomponentPhaseFluidBlastThermo`** (`src/thermodynamicModels/derived/multicomponentFluidBlastThermo/`):
  - Multicomponent phase fluid thermodynamics for multiphase systems (`phaseFluidBlastThermo`), so individual phases can consist of multiple chemical species with species transport and mixture properties.
- **`reactingPhaseFluidBlastThermo`** (`src/thermodynamicModels/derived/reactingPhaseFluidBlastThermo/`):
  - Pairs phase multicomponent thermodynamics with `blastCombustionModel` for per-phase chemical reactions.
  - Writes the phase heat release rate `Qdot.<phase>` ($[\text{W/m}^3]$, `AUTO_WRITE`), set from `combustion_->Qdot()` in `correct()`.

### 1.2 `eJWLC` Equation of State
- **Energy-based Mie-Grüneisen JWL with 3-term isentrope** (`src/thermodynamicModels/specie/equationOfStates/equationOfStates/eJWLC/`):
  - Full 3-term ($A, B, C, R_1, R_2, \omega$) isentrope pressure formulation:
    $$p(\rho, e) = A \exp(-R_1 V) + B \exp(-R_2 V) + C V^{-(\omega+1)} + \omega \rho \left(e - e_s(V)\right)$$
    where $V = \rho_0 / \rho$ and $e_s(V)$ is the isentrope energy (plus an optional constant `E0`).
  - Analytic CJ-isentrope reference temperature, with $V_\text{CJ} = \rho_0/\rho_\text{CJ}$:
    $$T_s(V) = T_\text{CJ} \left(\frac{V_\text{CJ}}{V}\right)^\omega$$
  - Requires `Cv`, `T_CJ` and `rho_CJ` in the dictionary. Gives isentropic and off-isentropic temperatures when coupled with `eConst` caloric models.
  - Optional `omegaT` (default `omega`, added 2026-10-10) is the exponent of $T_s(V)$. It changes only the energy–temperature relation, so $T_s$ can pass through both $T_\text{CJ}$ and the gas-EOS temperature at the transfer state without changing the pressure: $\omega_T = \ln(T_\text{CJ}/T_\text{ref})/\ln(V_\text{ref}/V_\text{CJ})$.
  - Registered in `src/thermodynamicModels/specie/include/forBlastGases.H` and `forDetBlastGases.H`.

### 1.2a `nasa9` Thermodynamics (2026-10-14, Phase C2)
- **`nasa9Thermo`** (`src/thermodynamicModels/specie/thermoModels/nasa9/`, selected as `thermo nasa9;`): NASA 9-coefficient polynomials (McBride, Zehe and Gordon 2002) with any number of temperature ranges (`Tswitch (200 1000 6000 20000)`, one `(a0 .. a6 b1 b2)` per range), constant Cp outside the overall range (as `janafBlastThermo`), mixtures by mass-weighted coefficients (all species of a mixture must share the ranges; checked). Instantiated for the coefficient gases (`forCoeffGasThermos`: all transports, perfectGas, AbelNobel, VanderWaals), i.e. the multicomponent and reacting gas phases. The existing `janaf9BlastThermo` has two ranges and is not instantiated for these phases.
- **`utilities/thermo_fitter/nasa9_species.py`**: generates the species dictionaries on the common ranges 200 - 1000 - 6000 - 20 000 K from the EuDetCode NASA-9 database (`external/eudetcode/Data/GasFlame.json`, copied exactly), Cantera NASA-7 data (Al, AlO; written exactly in NASA-9 form with a constant-Cp range above 6000 K, or fitted) and condensed species (C(gr), Al2O3(s): least-squares fit per range, continuous H and S). Ranges that do not join continuously are dropped: EuDetCode's 6000 - 20 000 K range of H2O, NH3 and CH4 is discontinuous (at 6000 K H2O's Cp/R drops from 7.52 to 1.78 and H/R jumps by 17 800 K), so these species use constant Cp above 6000 K. Covolume and transport of new species (O, N, NO, H, OH, AlO) are taken from a parent species.
- **Verification** (`tests/0D/verify_nasa9_thermo.py`, Tier 1, with `applications/test/Test-nasa9Thermo`): the compiled class reproduces the coefficients for 18 species and air to the output precision (≤ 4.6e-6) from 200 K to 25 000 K, and the source data exactly for the NASA-9 and NASA-7 species; the fitted species differ from their source by 0.5% (C(gr)), 0.6% (AlO) and 2.7% (Al2O3(s), at the oxide melting point 2327 K, where the solid data end).

### 1.2b Chemical Equilibrium Solver (2026-10-14, Phase C3)
- **`blastChemicalEquilibrium<ThermoType>`** (`src/thermodynamicModels/chemistryModel/equilibrium/`, header-only template): chemical equilibrium of a gas phase at fixed density and absolute internal energy (UV, `solveUV`) or temperature (TV, `solveTV`). Element-potential method: at fixed T and V the gas amounts are $n_k = V p^\circ/(R_u T)\exp(-g_k + \sum_i a_{ik}\lambda_i)$ (ideal-gas chemical potentials, $g_k = W_k G^\circ_k/(R_u T)$, $p^\circ$ = 1 bar), and $\lambda$ maximises the concave dual $b\cdot\lambda - \sum_k n_k(\lambda)$ subject to $a_c\cdot\lambda \le g_c$ for each condensed species (names with a phase suffix, e.g. C(gr), Al2O3(s), treated as pure phases; their amounts are the Lagrange multipliers). Newton iterations on the KKT system with an active set of condensed species, a backtracking line search on the Lagrangian, element scaling (trace elements are resolved) and an SVD pseudo-inverse with an absolute cut-off for directions not fixed by significant species (elements held in a condensed phase at low temperature). The temperature follows from a safeguarded Newton iteration on the equilibrium energy, with the analytic $dn/dT$. The species energies are those of the thermo model ($E_s + H_f$; for the Abel–Nobel gas $h - RT$, the covolume enters only the pressure), so the composition change conserves the flow solver's energy; elements are parsed from the species names or given in an `elements` dictionary.
- **Verification** (`tests/0D/verify_equilibrium.py`, Tier 1, with `applications/test/Test-blastEquilibrium`): air (N2 O2 NO N O), the gas part of the Comp-B/Al products with 1% Al vapour (16 species incl. Al, AlO, H, OH), its 50/50 and 20/80 mixtures with air, densities 0.01–100 kg/m³ and equilibrium temperatures 2500–20 000 K: agreement with EuDetCode (UV, ideal gas, same NASA-9 coefficients and 1 bar; driver `utilities/thermo_fitter/eudet_equilibrium/eudet_uv_equilibrium.cpp`) within 4e-4 in T and p and 0.4% in the major mass fractions, i.e. within EuDetCode's own energy residual (up to 3e-3); agreement with an independent Python solution (SLSQP on the dual, Brent on T) within 1e-6 for all states, including the products with C(gr) and Al2O3(s). Element balance ≤ 1e-14 (≤ 5e-13 at low temperature with condensed phases), energy to round-off; about 0.1–2 ms per state. EuDetCode's UV and TV solvers do not converge below 1e5 Pa (those states have no EuDetCode reference), its TV routine equilibrates at the pressure guess and keeps that composition, and a composition containing Al gets a default 90% inert-Al bound; the driver therefore passes element abundances directly and clears the bound.
- Known limitation: of 384 stress-test states (density 1e-3–3000 kg/m³, frozen temperature 300–60 000 K, compositions with trace elements, carbon or Al without oxygen, condensed-dominated mixtures), 6 do not converge, all with equilibrium temperatures below ~850 K (trace elements in room-temperature air, a dense C(gr)/Al2O3(s)/N2 mixture at 800 K); `solveUV` then returns false.

### 1.2c Equilibrium Gas Chemistry (2026-10-14, Phase C4)
- **Local chemical equilibrium in `standardBlastChemistryModel`**: with a sub-dictionary `equilibrium { Teq 2500; }` in the chemistry properties, cells above `Teq` are set to the equilibrium composition at their density and absolute internal energy (`blastChemicalEquilibrium`, Section 1.2b); the reaction rate is $\dot\omega_k = \rho (Y_{k,\text{eq}} - Y_k^0)/\Delta t$, so with $\dot Q = -\sum_k h_{f,k}\dot\omega_k$ mass, elements and energy are conserved and the equilibrium is reached in one time step. Below `Teq` the finite-rate mechanism is used. Above `Teq` the stiff ODE is never used: if the equilibrium solve fails or the equilibrium mixture is outside its equation of state (unlimited pressure ≤ 0, e.g. past the Abel–Nobel covolume, where the flow solver cannot invert the energy), the cell stays frozen; the log reports the cells per step (`Chemical equilibrium (T > Teq K): n cells[, m frozen ...]`).

- **ODE guard**: the rates and heat capacities in `derivatives()`/`jacobian()` are evaluated at $T \ge 200$ K; trial states of the stiff integrators with $T \le 0$ made the Arrhenius factor overflow (floating-point exception at 7.4 µs in the spherical case below `Teq`).

## 2. Finite Volume Models (`fvModels`) & Phase Change

### 2.1 Pressure-Driven Phase Change Model (`pressurePhaseChange`)
Location: `src/thermodynamicModels/fvModels/pressurePhaseChange/`, built into `libblastThermodynamics` (moved from `src/finiteVolume` in `c3294dc1`, see Section 5). Also selectable under the alias `pressurePhaseTransfer`.

- Pressure-induced mass transfer from a source phase to one or more multicomponent target phases, driven by a pressure interval instead of a temperature threshold. This avoids the known inaccuracy of empirical JWL temperature/caloric formulations.
- Interval input (first match wins): `pInterval (pMin pMax)`, `pMin`/`pMax`, `pLower`/`pUpper`, or `pThreshold` (upper bound; `pLower` optional, default 0). A legacy `Tthreshold` entry gives a warning and falls back to $[0, 100\,\text{bar}]$.
- Transition factor, for $p_\text{lower} > 0$:
  $$\Gamma(p) = \tfrac{1}{4}\left(1 + \tanh\tfrac{p_\text{upper} - p}{p_\text{range}}\right)\left(1 + \tanh\tfrac{p - p_\text{lower}}{p_\text{range}}\right)\,H\!\left(p_\text{upper} + 2p_\text{range} - p\right)\,H\!\left(p - p_\text{lower} + 2p_\text{range}\right)$$
  where $H$ is a hard step (`pos`). For $p_\text{lower} \le 0$ the one-sided form $\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1 + \tanh\tfrac{p_\text{upper} - p}{p_\text{range}}\right)H(p_\text{upper} + 2p_\text{range} - p)$ is used.
  - $p_\text{range}$ defaults to $\max(0.05\,(p_\text{upper} - p_\text{lower}),\,1\,\text{Pa})$; an explicit `pRange` (or `Prange`) overrides it.
- Transfer rate $\dot m = \Gamma\,\alpha_s \rho_s/\tau$ (`tau`, default $10^{-4}$ s). Since `0a181853`, if a `lambda.<sourcePhase>` field exists (programmed burn), $\dot m$ is multiplied by it in the mass-source path. (2026-10-15) And it is zero where $\lambda <$ `lambdaMin` (default 0.99): in the burn front $\lambda > 0$ before the pressure has risen above the threshold, so with a fast transfer ($\tau$ = 0.1–1 µs, threshold ≥ 200 bar) unreacted charge was moved into the gas at the charge density (720–1600 kg/m³) and an energy no gas state has; the gas temperature inversion then diverged (the three aborted runs of the B3 sweep). The default case (100 bar, 20 µs) is unchanged to 0.001 MPa at the gauges.
- `targetFractions` sets the target-phase species distribution, as mass fractions by default. With `isMoleFraction true` they are converted on first use to mass fractions, $Y_i = X_i W_i/\sum_j X_j W_j$, using the molecular weights of the target-phase thermo. The converted values are printed to the log. A target specie that is not in the phase, or a target phase without multicomponent thermo, is a fatal error.
- Energy and momentum: all phases share an energy datum, reconciled through the products `E0` (offline, `utilities/thermo_fitter`). An optional constant datum shift `eOffset` [J/kg] (default 0) can be added to the transferred mass. No state-dependent offset is computed (see Section 7).
  - blastEulerFoam (multi-fluid, per-phase `alphaRho`, `alphaRhoU`, `alphaRhoE`; since 2026-10-10): the transferred mass carries the source-phase velocity $\mathbf{u}_s$ and specific total energy $E_s = (\alpha\rho E)_s/(\alpha\rho)_s$. The source loses $\dot m(1, \mathbf{u}_s, E_s)$; each target gains $f_k\dot m(1, \mathbf{u}_s, E_s + e_\text{off})$. Volume moves with the mass ($\pm\dot m/\rho_s$), so internal (not enthalpy) energy is carried. Mass, momentum and datum-corrected energy are conserved exactly.
  - blastFoam (single mixture energy and velocity): `rhoE` source $\dot m\,e_\text{off}$ only.
- Note: the code was introduced in `4cb49946` as a new model. No `temperaturePhaseChange` model was ever committed to this repository's history.

### 4.6 Flux Schemes
- **`HLLC` phase flux scheme** (`src/finiteVolume/fluxSchemes/phaseFluxSchemes/HLLC/`):
  - Rewritten to the standard HLLC star-state flux $F + S(U^* - U)$ with separate owner/neighbour star pressures.
  - Explicit $S_\text{nei} \le 0$ branch, guarded $S^*$, and central-average fallback.
  - A phase absent on one side of a face ($\alpha \le 10^{-6}$) is treated as vacuum (upstream). (2026-10-13) Tried: giving the absent side the state of the present side. This removes the start-up transient of the stationary charge/air interface (pressure stays at 1.01325 bar, velocities below 0.1 m/s for 40 µs), but at a detonating contact the products' face pressure flux then carries their full pressure work into the air cell while the balancing $P_I\nabla\alpha$ term there uses the air pressure, and the trace products reach 1e5 K (the spherical cases fail at 9–11 µs). Not adopted; see Section 7.
  - (2026-10-14) **Trace sides take the mixture impedance** (`alphaTrace`, `phaseProperties`, default 0: off). Where a fluid phase is a trace on one side of a face ($\alpha < $ `alphaTrace`, blended linearly in $\alpha$/`alphaTrace`; at $\alpha \le 10^{-6}$ in place of the vacuum state), the wave speeds and a common star pressure of that phase's Riemann problem use the cell's mixture density, frozen sound speed $\sqrt{\sum\alpha_k\rho_k c_k^2/\sum\alpha_k\rho_k}$, velocity and pressure on that side; the star flux is written directly as $\rho^* S^* U^* + p^* n$ and $(\rho E^* + p^*)S^*$. The phase's own density and energy still define the fluxes, so they stay conservative; where the phase is above `alphaTrace` on both sides the flux is unchanged. Before, a trace of unreacted explosive in the first air cell had the explosive's impedance (4.5e6 Pa·s/m): when the air there moved outwards by 0.015 m/s, the explosive face pressure dropped by 0.35 bar, and with the upwind face volume fraction switching from the trace to 0.818 this pulled the air cell back by 0.6 m/s in one step (the interface instability, Section 7). The mixture state comes from `phaseSystem` through `phaseFluxScheme::setMixture()`; only `HLLC` uses it.
  - (2026-10-14, later) **No flux from a residual upwind side with `alphaTrace`**: a side with $\alpha \le 10^{-6}$ has zero density, but with the trace impedance its star pressure is the mixture pressure, so the star-region energy and momentum fluxes carried a pressure-work term without mass into the neighbouring trace cell. When a trace gas cell next to the products became residual, its neighbour's gas jumped to the 50 000 K decode cap and oscillated between the cap and the 200 K floor for several steps. The mass, momentum and energy fluxes from a residual upwind side are now zero (the face `alpha` for the volume-fraction transport is unchanged). Cap hits in the inert spherical case 11 → 3; peak/breakout/air-shock pressures, Al velocities and the interface test unchanged. Without `alphaTrace` the scheme is unchanged.
- (2026-10-20) **Hybrid HLLC/HLL** (`hybridHLL` in `fluxSchemes.<phase>` of `fvSchemes`; default yes since 2026-10-20, `hybridHLL no` restores the plain HLLC). Near shocks the mass, momentum and energy fluxes of the star region are blended towards the HLL flux with the weight $1 - f^3$, $f$ the smallest pressure ratio $\min(p_i/p_j, p_j/p_i)$ between a cell of the face and its neighbours (the sensor of the single-phase `HLLCP`, Xie et al. 2018; mixture pressure with `alphaTrace`, processor neighbours included). Faces with a trace or absent side keep the HLLC flux; the volume flux, face volume fraction and face pressure stay HLLC. The HLL dissipation of the contact and shear waves removes the carbuncle of the HLLC flux at strong shocks aligned with the mesh: in the 2D axisymmetric spherical charge (`tests/2D_axi`, plan step G1) the rows of cells along the axis and the symmetry plane had peaks up to 9% below and arrival 1% ahead of the other directions, and a trace of products ran along both up to the shock (7 mm ahead of the contact elsewhere at 30 µs). With `hybridHLL` on the gas and the products the contact is at 150.5–152.0 mm in all directions at 26 µs and the shock radius uniform to 0.45%; the axis and plane peaks at 0.15 m are within the range of the other directions. It raises the air-shock peaks by 1–2% in 1D and by 2.5–3% in 2D (0.5 mm), within the resolution change of the 1D case (0.25 mm: −3.4 / −1.2 / +1.0% at 80 / 100 / 150 mm). Made the default (2026-10-20), so all blastEulerFoam cases with the phase HLLC use it. Full test suite with the default: 30/31 pass, the new 2D test passing on its own (the runner timeout was too short); in the 1D spherical Comp-B/Al cases breakout 2.79–2.81 GPa (2.94 with HLLC), air shock in the reacting case 37 / 28 / 18 MPa at 80 / 100 / 150 mm (36 / 27 / 17), detonation peak 15.2 GPa (15.8), closed-domain energy −3.43e-4 / −3.82e-4 / −2.60e-4 (−3.35e-4 / −3.75e-4 / −2.54e-4); gunIB1D and the 0D tests unchanged. The E2 runs of the 2 m tutorial and the 2D tutorial run were made with plain HLLC and were not repeated.
- `HLL`, `HLLC` and `Kurganov` phase flux schemes are also registered in the `solid` selection table.
- **`fluxScheme`**: Overloaded `NewSingle(phi, type)` and `NewMulti(phi, type)` factory methods for selecting a scheme by name. Not yet called anywhere.

### 4.8 Adaptive Mesh Refinement (2026-10-21)
- **Coarsening above a decreasing maximum level** (`polyMeshHexRefiner`): with a `maxRefinement` that decreases with time (a `Function1` table) the cells above the new maximum were refined no further but were coarsened only where the error was below `unrefineLevel`, so the refined shock and fireball kept the old level. Cells above their maximum level are now unprotected and taken as below `unrefineLevel` in the unrefinement step (one level per refinement interval, buffer layers kept).
- **`errorEstimator`**: the per-cell maximum level of several estimators was set with `(maxLevel[celli], level)` (comma operator, the last estimator won) instead of `max(...)`.
- **`decomposePar` and `refinementHistory`**: `decomposePar` does not decompose the `refinementHistory`, so cells refined before decomposition (`setRefinedFields`, mapped fields) can never be coarsened in the parallel run. Cases that refine the initial fields for a parallel run do so after `decomposePar` (as `ChargeCompBAl_2D_axi_2m`, Section 6.1, with `rotateFields -refine` in parallel). In `tests/2D_axi` the charge cells refined by `setRefinedFields` stay refined (harmless, the charge is in the refined region until the end).
- **`linearCombination` error estimator** (2026-10-22, `src/errorEstimators/linearCombination`): the error is a weighted sum of fields, optionally divided by a second weighted sum (`fields`, `denominator`); cells between `lowerRefineLevel` and `upperRefineLevel` are refined, below `unrefineLevel` or above `upperUnrefineLevel` coarsened. With the element mass fractions of the gas species (carbon / (carbon + nitrogen), 0.449 in the Comp-B/Al products and 0 in air) it marks the products/air mixing layer independently of the chemistry and of the Al oxides (carbon alone drops by 2% where the Al burns).
- **Refinement criteria for the fireball**: `densityGradient` keeps the whole mixed fireball refined (74 000 cells at 60 µs in the 2 m case); `scaledDelta` on `rho` and `p` (0.2 / 0.1) and on `alpha.Al` (`minValue 1e-3`, 0.3 / 0.1) refines the shock, the contact and the Al cloud edge (23 000 cells).

### Open
- **Carbuncle at the axis and the symmetry plane in 2D** (2026-10-20, resolved by the hybrid flux): with the plain HLLC flux the strong air shock aligned with the mesh is unstable along the axis and the symmetry plane of the 2D axisymmetric spherical case, on the uniform mesh as well as with AMR: peaks up to 9% below (axis) and 6% below / 2% above (plane) the other directions, arrival on the axis 1% ahead, and from 14 µs a trace of products running along both up to the shock. The hybrid HLLC/HLL flux (`hybridHLL`, Section 4.6, default since 2026-10-20) removes it, at the cost of 1–3% higher air-shock peaks at 0.5 mm. Remaining: on the axis at 80 mm the peak is still 15% high (early, near the charge), and a blend only on faces transverse to the shock would avoid the peak increase.
- **Axis and symmetry plane in the 2D 2 m case** (2026-10-22, plan step G2b): with the mixing layer resolved, the inward secondary shock runs ahead along the axis and the symmetry plane from 0.2–0.5 ms; air enters the fireball along the axis and a products jet runs out along the plane. Affects the 0° and 90° probes and the mixing there; cause (flux, gradients or mesh alignment at the axis) not yet found.
- **Products/air mixing layer not refined in the 2D 2 m case** (2026-10-21, plan step G2; resolved 2026-10-22 by the `linearCombination` criterion, Section 4.8): the `scaledDelta` criteria on `rho` and `p` (20% jumps between neighbours) refine the mixing layer only while it is sharp; from 0.5 ms it lies on the 16 mm base cells, 3–4 times wider than in 1D, and the extra numerical mixing burns 14% more CO than the 1D case (peaks up to 9%, impulse up to 27% higher). Needs a criterion on the products or species fractions, or across several cells, before the mixing studies (G3).
- **Energy of the particle coupling with small particles** (2026-10-19): with the 30 µm Al of the spherical tests (1 mm before) the closed-domain energy of the inert case changes by −1e-4 at breakout and then by about −1e-5 per µs (−3.35e-4 by 37 µs; −3.75e-4 with transfer and chemistry), while the products accelerate and heat the Al (Al kinetic plus thermal energy +11% of the total by 35 µs, i.e. the error is about 0.3% of the energy exchanged). With 1 mm Al it was 5e-5. Not the cold compression energy of the Al (its change, −5e-3, is converted to kinetic energy by the pressure work). Suspected: the discretisation of the pressure work on the particle volume and of the drag work (Section 3.3, A6), which become large with strongly coupled particles. The spherical verifiers allow 5e-4 and end the energy window when the Al cloud, which outruns the shock with 30 µm particles, reaches the boundary.
